"Smart Christmas lights", "app-controlled Christmas lights" and "programmable Christmas lights" get used as if they meant the same thing. They do not, and the difference decides what your lights can actually do.

## Three levels of smart

| Level | What you control | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Smart plug | On/off and timers only | Ordinary fairy lights on a smart plug |
| App-controlled colour | Colour, brightness and preset effects for the whole string | Most "smart" RGB strings |
| Programmable / addressable (pixel) | Each LED separately, so you can draw shapes, text and animations | Pixel strings and pixel curtains |

If a listing says "DIY pattern", "pixel", "addressable" or lets you draw in the app, it is the third kind: programmable Christmas lights. Twinkly lights belong here too; see our [Twinkly vs Govee guide]({{post:twinkly-vs-govee-curtain-lights}}).

## How pixel (addressable) LEDs work

Every LED has a tiny chip with an address. The controller sends a frame of colours many times per second, and each LED shows its own colour. Arrange the LEDs in a grid and you have a low-resolution screen. That is how pixel Christmas lights in a curtain of a few hundred LEDs can show Father Christmas waving.

Two practical consequences:

- **The shape matters.** A string round a tree shows colour sweeps; a flat grid such as a curtain can show recognisable pictures and words.
- **More LEDs means more detail**, but even 400 LEDs in a grid is enough for clear Christmas pictures from across the road.

## Smart Christmas lights outdoors

Outdoors, check three things before the app features:

1. **Water rating** – IP44 for covered spots, IP65+ where the rain hits. UK winters are wet rather than snowy.
2. **Where the controller lives** – it should sit somewhere dry, ideally indoors.
3. **Connection** – Bluetooth works within about 10 m of your phone; Wi-Fi lets you control the lights from anywhere but needs a signal at the window or balcony.

## UK plugs, power and safety

- **Mains lights** need a 230 V rating and a fused UK three-pin plug (Type G, BS 1363), not a travel adaptor.
- **USB lights** run from a USB wall adapter with a UK plug. Electrical Safety First calls extra-low-voltage lights the safest option.
- **Outdoor sockets** need 30 mA RCD protection under the wiring regulations (BS 7671).
- **Look for UKCA or CE marking** and buy from a reputable seller.

## Smart Christmas light terms, explained

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| RGB | Each LED can mix red, green and blue to show almost any colour. |
| Addressable / pixel | Each LED can be set separately, so the lights can show shapes and text. |
| Controller | The small box that stores effects and sends data to the LEDs. |
| Bluetooth | Short-range control from a phone in the same room or nearby. |
| Wi-Fi | Control from anywhere, as long as the lights have a signal. |
| IP rating | How well the lights resist dust and water (IP44 = splash resistant). |
| Music sync | A mode where the lights react to sound from a microphone or the app. |

## What can go wrong with smart lights

- **The app stops being updated.** Check that the app is current in your phone's app store before you buy.
- **Range problems.** Bluetooth lights on a balcony may not respond from the back of the flat.
- **Too many apps.** Lights from different brands usually need separate apps and do not work together.
- **Power cuts reset some controllers.** With dusk around 4 pm, a timer that survives one is a plus.

## Which should you buy?

- Want a tree that changes colour? An app-controlled RGB string is enough.
- Want pictures, words and animations in a window or on a balcony? Buy a **pixel curtain**.
- Want the lights to dance to songs? Start with [music sync]({{post:christmas-lights-to-music}}).
- Want a full synchronised house show with dozens of props? That is the hobbyist world of dedicated controllers and sequencing software, which is a bigger project.
