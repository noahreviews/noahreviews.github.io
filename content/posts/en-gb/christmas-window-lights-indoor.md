Christmas window lights are the easiest and safest way to light up your home for the street: no ladders, no weatherproofing and no cold, wet fingers. Whether you live in a terrace, a semi or a flat, the trick is making lights that sit indoors look good from outside.

## Ideas for Christmas window lights indoors

1. **Curtain lights behind the glass.** A sheet of light strands covers the whole window and is visible from down the road. Our [Christmas curtain lights guide]({{post:christmas-curtain-lights}}) covers sizes and power.
2. **Window fairy lights** around the frame, outlining the glass without blocking the view.
3. **A star or snowflake light.** One big shape per window, centred, is classic and calm.
4. **Silhouette window lights** (reindeer, sleigh, candles) on suction cups, or **electric candles** on the sill for a traditional look.
5. **An animated pixel curtain** that shows pictures and text, for a window people stop to look at.

## How to make indoor lights visible from outside

- **Get close to the glass.** Lights a few centimetres from the window show clearly; lights a metre back mostly light your room.
- **Go in front of the nets.** Hang the lights between any net curtain and the glass, or the fabric blurs the display.
- **Darken what is behind them.** Close the curtains or blinds behind the display, or switch off the room light.
- **Use the right brightness.** Too bright and you get glare and reflections; around 50–70% brightness usually looks best at night.
- **Think about the angle.** For upstairs windows and flats, people look up, so centre the display in the lower half of the window.

## Common mistakes with window lights

- **Lights too far from the glass.** The display looks faint from outside and lights up your room instead.
- **A bright lamp behind the display.** It washes out the picture; switch it off or close the curtains behind the lights.
- **Full brightness at night.** It causes glare and reflections; turn it down in the evening.
- **Blocking the window you need to open.** Leave one free for airing the room, or use lights that clip to the frame and lift away.

## Power and safety indoors

- USB-powered lights (5 V) are the simplest indoors, stay cool and run from any USB adapter with a UK plug.
- UK radiators often sit under the window; keep cables, controllers and curtains clear of the heat.
- Winter condensation can wet the sill, so keep plugs and controllers off it.
- Use a timer so the display runs from dusk, around 4 pm in December, until bedtime. Electrical Safety First advises unplugging Christmas lights when you go out and overnight, and not overloading sockets.

## Measure your window

Measure the inside width of the window recess and the height from the top of the frame to the sill. For curtain lights, pick a size close to the width so the strands hang straight. For a single shape such as a star, a size around a third of the window width looks balanced.

## Window light ideas by room

- **Front room:** a curtain light or an animated display, as this is usually the biggest street-facing window.
- **Bedroom:** a soft warm-white star or a slow twinkle; avoid fast animations if you can see them from bed.
- **Kitchen:** a simple silhouette or battery candles on the sill, away from the hob and the sink.
- **Children's rooms:** battery lights with a timer, and cables kept out of reach.
