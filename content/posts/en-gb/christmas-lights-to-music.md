The houses you see online flashing in time to Slade or Mariah Carey rely on hours of hand-made sequencing. You do not need that for musical Christmas lights that move with whatever you play. There are three levels, and two of them take minutes. (In UK shops, the term can also mean a set that plays its own carols; more on those below.)

## Option 1: Lights with a built-in microphone

Many RGB fairy lights and curtain lights have a **music mode**: a small microphone in the controller listens for the beat and changes colour or pattern with the volume.

- **Cost:** nothing extra.
- **Effort:** press a button.
- **Result:** the lights react to whatever is playing in the room. They follow beats, not specific notes, so it feels like a party rather than a choreographed show.

## Option 2: App music sync

Some smart lights use your phone's microphone or the music playing on your phone. The app analyses the sound and sends effects to the lights.

- **Cost:** included with app-controlled lights.
- **Effort:** a few taps.
- **Result:** similar to the microphone mode, but often with more effect choices and better sensitivity.

## Option 3: Full sequencing

Hobbyists use pixel controllers and free software such as xLights to programme every light to a song, frame by frame. Visitors tune their car radio to a small FM transmitter to hear the music; check that any transmitter is legal to use in the UK.

- **Cost:** controllers, power supplies, lots of pixels.
- **Effort:** many hours per song.
- **Result:** the choreographed shows you see online.

## Set up music sync in five steps

1. Hang the lights and check they work in a normal mode first.
2. Open the app, or press the music button on the controller or remote.
3. Put the controller or phone near the speaker, smart speaker or soundbar.
4. Play a song with a clear beat and adjust the sensitivity until the lights pulse without flickering constantly.
5. Try the different music patterns and pick the one that suits the song.

## The three options compared

| | Built-in microphone | App sync | Full sequencing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup time | Seconds | Minutes | Hours per song |
| Extra hardware | None | None | Controllers, power supplies, lots of pixels |
| Follows the exact song | No, reacts to beat and volume | No, reacts to sound | Yes, frame by frame |
| Best for | Parties, windows, balconies | Parties with more effect choice | Large outdoor shows |

## Choosing musical Christmas lights

- **More individually controlled LEDs** give smoother, more varied patterns than a few colour zones.
- **A flat layout** such as a curtain shows patterns more clearly than a string wrapped round a tree.
- **Look for adjustable sensitivity**, so you can tune it to quiet carols or a loud party playlist.
- **Check where the microphone is.** If it is in the controller, the controller needs to sit near the speaker.

## Lights that play their own tunes

Some cheap musical Christmas lights have a small speaker in the plug-in box that plays a loop of carols as the lights flash. Fun in a child's bedroom, but the tunes are fixed, so check you can mute them, and look for UKCA or CE marking.

## Which one is right for you?

If you want your window or balcony to dance while you have friends round, options 1 and 2 are plenty. Start with lights that already have music sync.

## Tips for better music sync

- Put the controller or phone close to the speaker, not across the room.
- Turn the sensitivity down if the lights flicker non-stop.
- Pick bass-heavy songs for clear pulses. Quiet carols give gentler movement.
- Think of the neighbours, especially in a flat: music modes for parties, calm scenes for late evening.
