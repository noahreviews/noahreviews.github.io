Christmas curtain lights are one of the easiest ways to make a window or balcony look decorated from the street: one horizontal wire with many thin strands hanging down, each strand carrying small LEDs. Hang the top wire, let the strands drop, and a whole surface lights up.

This guide covers what actually matters when you buy a set, whether it is for a **window indoors**, an **outdoor balcony railing**, or a patio door.

## Plain curtains vs. animated (pixel) curtains

There are two very different products sold under the same name:

| Type | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Plain curtain lights | Every LED shows the same color (often warm white), with a few flashing modes | Soft background glow, bedrooms, photo backdrops |
| Animated / pixel curtain lights | Each LED is individually addressable, so the curtain can show pictures, text and animations | Windows and balconies you want people to notice from the street |

If you want a Santa, a snowman or a "Merry Christmas" message on your window, you need the **individually addressable** kind (see our guide to [animated Christmas lights]({{post:animated-christmas-lights}})). The listing will usually mention an app, "pixel", "programmable" or "DIY pattern".

## Size and LED count

The two numbers to compare are **width × height** and **total LEDs**.

- A 6 ft 7 in × 6 ft 7 in (2 × 2 m) curtain covers a standard window or a short balcony section.
- A 9 ft 10 in × 9 ft 10 in (3 × 3 m) curtain covers a patio door or a wider railing.
- For animated curtains, more LEDs per square foot means a sharper picture. With the same number of LEDs, a smaller curtain looks more detailed and a larger one looks bigger but blockier from close up. From the street, both read well.

Measure the width you want to cover first. Curtain lights look best hanging flat, not bunched up, so pick the size closest to your window or railing rather than the biggest one.

## Indoor vs. outdoor

Christmas curtain lights **for windows** can live indoors, hanging behind the glass and facing out. That is the safest setup and the picture is still clearly visible from outside at night.

For **outdoor** use, check the water rating:

- **IP44** means splash resistant. Fine for a covered balcony or porch where rain does not hit the controller directly.
- **IP65** and above handle direct rain.

Whatever the rating, keep the controller and the plug dry and off the ground.

## Power: USB vs. plug-in (120 V)

Many modern curtain lights run on **5 V USB**. A USB set works the same in the US and abroad: only the wall adapter differs, so you avoid voltage and plug-type issues entirely. That is convenient indoors (any phone charger works) and very safe, but check whether a USB power adapter is included. Some sets ship with only the USB cable. Sets that plug straight into a standard 120 V outlet are usually brighter and longer, but outdoors they need a GFCI-protected outlet, which the National Electrical Code (NEC 210.8) requires for outdoor receptacles. On any plug-in set, look for a UL or ETL listing mark.

## How to hang curtain lights on a window

For more window ideas, see [indoor Christmas window lights]({{post:christmas-window-lights-indoor}}).

1. Clip the top wire to the curtain rail, or use clear adhesive hooks along the top of the window frame.
2. Let the strands drop straight. Do not stretch them, or the picture on an animated curtain will distort.
3. Push the lights close to the glass. The closer they are, the less reflection you get inside the room.
4. Hide the controller on the windowsill and run the cable to an outlet.

## Buying checklist for Christmas curtain lights

Before you order, check these points on the listing:

1. **Width and height** match the window, door or railing you want to cover.
2. **LED type**: plain (one color at a time) or addressable pixels (pictures and text).
3. **Water rating**: IP44 for covered spots, IP65 or higher for direct rain.
4. **Power**: USB or 120 V plug-in, and whether the adapter you need is in the box.
5. **Control**: remote, app, or both, and whether the app has a timer.
6. **Cable length** from the controller to the nearest outlet.
7. **Returns and warranty**, in case the size turns out wrong.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Buying the biggest size.** A curtain that is too wide bunches up and the picture distorts.
- **Hanging it far from the glass.** You mostly light your own room and get reflections.
- **Forgetting the adapter.** Many USB sets ship with a cable only.
- **Leaving the controller in the rain.** The strands may be splash resistant while the controller box is not.

## Storing curtain lights after Christmas

Lay the curtain flat, fold the strands together in one direction, and roll the bundle around a piece of cardboard before it goes back into the box. Keep the controller and remote in a separate bag. Stored this way, the strands do not tangle and the set is ready to hang again next year.
