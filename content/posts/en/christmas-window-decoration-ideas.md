Windows are the easiest place to start decorating for the holidays: you see them from the couch, and your neighbors see them from the sidewalk. These **Christmas window decoration ideas** cover both views, from fairy lights and animated scenes to paper stars, garlands and a nativity window, with notes on safety and renter-friendly hanging. Most take an evening, and none of them need a ladder outside.

## Christmas window light ideas

### 1. Fairy lights behind a sheer curtain

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-sheer-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-sheer-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-sheer-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Warm white fairy lights glowing behind a sheer white curtain on a cozy window" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


Hang a string of warm white fairy lights, or a plain curtain light, on the curtain rod first, then draw a sheer curtain in front of it. The fabric softens the bulbs into a glow that looks cozy from the room and gives the window a gentle shimmer from outside. It also hides the wires. Pick a steady or slow-fade mode rather than fast flashing so the effect stays calm.

### 2. A curtain of lights close to the glass

For a window that reads clearly from the street, hang a curtain light an inch or two (a few centimeters) from the glass and keep the room behind it dark. A size close to the window width lets the strands hang straight. Our [indoor Christmas window lights guide]({{post:christmas-window-lights-indoor}}) explains brightness, reflections and sizing in more detail.

### 3. An animated LED curtain scene

Pixel curtains have individually controlled LEDs that draw moving pictures and text, such as falling snow, a snowman or a "Merry Christmas" message, so one window becomes something people slow down to watch. Turn the brightness down at night so the picture stays sharp instead of glaring.

### 4. Battery candles or a candle bridge

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-candle-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-candle-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-candle-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Wooden candle bridge with electric candles on a windowsill at dusk" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


A single electric candle in each window is a long-standing tradition in many American homes, and it looks especially neat on a house with a row of matching windows. A candle bridge, the arch of candles popular in Scandinavia and Germany, makes a lovely centerpiece on a wide sill. Choose battery or plug-in LED versions with a built-in timer; real flames do not belong in a window next to curtains (see the safety section below).

### 5. A paper star lantern

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-star-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-star-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-star-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="White paper star lantern glowing in a window" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


In Sweden, a paper Advent star glowing in the window is a classic, and the many-pointed Moravian star is a similar idea. Hang one star centered in the window, at a height people see from the sidewalk. Use the low-heat LED bulb that comes with the lantern, or an LED replacement, and keep the paper clear of the bulb.

## Christmas window decoration ideas without lights

### 6. Silhouettes and paper cutouts

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-silhouette-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-silhouette-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-silhouette-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Window with black paper silhouettes of reindeer, trees and houses, seen from a snowy street" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


Black paper silhouettes of a reindeer, a sleigh, a village skyline or a row of trees look sharp by day and stand out against a lit room at night. Print a template, trace it onto black card stock and cut it out with a craft knife. A skyline strip along the bottom of the glass is one of the simplest Christmas window ideas, and it works on any window size. Attach the pieces with small loops of removable tape.

### 7. Garlands and swags

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-garland-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-garland-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-garland-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Evergreen garland swag with red bows across the top of a window" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


A pine garland draped across the top of the frame, with the ends trailing down the sides, frames the window like a picture. Faux garlands last the whole season and do not drop needles. Tuck in pine cones, ribbon or a few ornaments that match your tree. For an even swag, hang it from three removable hooks: one at each top corner and one in the middle.

### 8. A wreath in every window

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-wreath-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-wreath-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-wreath-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Brick townhouse with a wreath in each front window" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


A wreath hung inside each street-facing window looks tidy and traditional, and matching sizes and ribbon colors make the whole house look planned. On a double-hung window, run the ribbon over the top of the upper sash and secure it behind; on other windows, use a removable hook on the frame or a suction-cup hook on the glass for small, light wreaths.

### 9. Paper stars and snowflakes

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-snowflakes-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-snowflakes-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-snowflakes-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Paper snowflakes and stars hanging on strings in a bright window" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


Cut paper snowflakes cost almost nothing: fold a square of paper into a triangle, snip shapes along the edges and unfold. Folded paper stars, such as German Fröbel stars or simple origami stars, hung on clear thread at different heights look like falling snow. White paper shows up best against a dark window in the evening.

### 10. Frosted corners with snow spray

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="/images/ideas/n-frosted-1200.jpg" srcset="/images/ideas/n-frosted-800.jpg 800w, /images/ideas/n-frosted-1200.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="900" alt="Window panes with frosted snow-spray corners and silver baubles" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


Aerosol snow spray gives the glass a wintry edge. Spray lightly into the bottom corners, or hold a paper stencil of a snowflake, a star or the word "Noel" against the glass for crisp shapes. Ventilate the room, follow the directions on the can and check how it is removed before you start. Reusable gel or static window clings are a mess-free alternative, especially with kids.

## Ideas for families and faith

### 11. A nativity window

<figure class="idea-img"><img src="https://lumalyn.store/cdn/shop/files/lumalyn-christian-s3-show.jpg?width=1200" srcset="https://lumalyn.store/cdn/shop/files/lumalyn-christian-s3-show.jpg?width=800 800w, https://lumalyn.store/cdn/shop/files/lumalyn-christian-s3-show.jpg?width=1200 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 760px) 100vw, 720px" width="1200" height="1600" alt="Window showing a glowing nativity scene in lights, seen from the street" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>


A nativity silhouette, the Holy Family under a star, is one of the most striking window designs because the simple outline reads clearly from the street. ### 12. Kid-made decorations

Let children own one window. Washable window markers, paper chains, handprint wreaths and gel clings are easy to make and easy to change. Hang their art low on the glass so it shows from outside as well as at their eye level. Keep cords out of reach, and skip anything with small button batteries for young children.

### 13. A window Advent calendar

In parts of Germany, Austria and Switzerland, neighbors take turns decorating a window for each day of Advent. Make your own version: number 24 paper cutouts or envelopes and reveal one each day, or add one new decoration every evening until Christmas Eve.

## Ideas that pull the whole look together

### 14. Match the view from outside and inside

Your windows are seen from two sides at different times: from the street mostly after dark, and from inside mostly in daylight. Choose one light color (warm white or cool white) for every front window, echo an outdoor wreath with a garland inside, and repeat one accent color. Then cross the street one evening and look back. You will spot reflections, visible cords and dark gaps that you cannot see from the room.

### 15. A renter-friendly, no-drill setup

- **Tension rods** fitted inside the window frame hold lights, a garland or a light swag without screws.
- **Removable adhesive hooks** rated above the weight of the decoration; peel them off slowly with the release tab, along the wall rather than outward.
- **Suction cups** hold light items on clean, dry glass.
- **Painter's tape and static clings** instead of nails or thumbtacks.

## Plan your window in four steps

1. **Decide who it is for.** A street-facing living room window wants bold shapes and lights near the glass; a kitchen or bedroom window can be softer and more personal.
2. **Measure the glass.** Note the inside width and height of the frame before you buy a curtain light, a garland or a wreath, so nothing sags, bunches or blocks the window you open for fresh air.
3. **Choose a palette.** Two or three colors, such as green, red and warm white, or white, silver and blue, look calmer than every color at once.
4. **Plan the power.** Find the nearest outlet first, and favor battery or USB-powered pieces where a cord would cross a walkway.

## Christmas window decoration safety

- **No real candles near curtains.** NFPA advises keeping candles at least 12 inches (30 cm) from anything that can burn, and both NFPA and the American Red Cross suggest flameless candles. In a window full of fabric, paper and greenery, battery or LED candles are the sensible choice.
- **Use lights where they are rated.** Some lights are made for indoor or outdoor use only, not both. For a porch, a window box or anything outside the glass, use outdoor-rated lights and extension cords, plug into a GFCI-protected outlet and keep plugs and connections off wet sills.
- **Hang with clips, not nails,** so cords are not damaged, and replace any light string with worn cords or loose bulbs.
- **Switch displays off when you leave or go to bed.** A timer or app schedule makes it automatic.
- **Mind the heat.** Keep lights, garlands and paper decorations away from space heaters and radiators.
