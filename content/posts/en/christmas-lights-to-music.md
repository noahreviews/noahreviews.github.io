The famous houses that flash in time to Trans-Siberian Orchestra use hours of hand-made sequencing. You do not need that to get lights that move with music. There are three levels.

## Option 1: Lights with a built-in microphone

Many RGB strings and curtains have a **music mode**: a small microphone in the controller listens for beats and changes color or pattern with the volume.

- **Cost:** nothing extra.
- **Effort:** press a button.
- **Result:** the lights react to whatever is playing in the room. They react to beats, not to specific notes, so it feels like a party rather than a choreographed show.

## Option 2: App music sync

Some smart lights use your phone's microphone or the music playing on your phone. The app analyzes the sound and sends effects to the lights.

- **Cost:** included with app-controlled lights.
- **Effort:** a few taps.
- **Result:** similar to the microphone mode, but often with more effect choices and better sensitivity.

## Option 3: Full sequencing

Hobbyists use pixel controllers and software such as xLights to program every light to a song, frame by frame. Visitors tune a car radio to a small FM transmitter to hear the music.

- **Cost:** controllers, power supplies, many pixels.
- **Effort:** many hours per song.
- **Result:** the "wow" choreographed shows you see online.

## Set up music sync in five steps

1. Hang the lights and confirm they work in a normal mode first.
2. Open the app or press the music button on the controller or remote.
3. Place the controller or phone near the speaker.
4. Play a song with a clear beat and adjust the sensitivity until the lights pulse without flickering constantly.
5. Try the different music patterns and pick the one that suits the song.

## The three options compared

| | Built-in microphone | App sync | Full sequencing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup time | Seconds | Minutes | Hours per song |
| Extra hardware | None | None | Controllers, power supplies, many pixels |
| Follows the exact song | No, reacts to beat and volume | No, reacts to sound | Yes, frame by frame |
| Best for | Parties, windows, balconies | Parties with more effect choice | Large outdoor shows |

## Choosing lights for music sync

- **More individually controlled LEDs** give smoother, more varied patterns than a few color zones.
- **A flat layout** such as a curtain shows patterns more clearly than a string wrapped around a tree.
- **Look for adjustable sensitivity**, so you can tune it to quiet carols or loud party music.
- **Check where the microphone is.** If it is in the controller, the controller needs to be near the speaker.

## Which one is right for you?

If you want your window or balcony to dance while you have friends over, options 1 and 2 are plenty. Start with lights that already have music sync.

## Tips for better music sync

- Put the controller or phone close to the speaker, not across the room.
- Turn the sensitivity down if the lights flicker non-stop.
- Pick bass-heavy songs for clear pulses. Quiet carols give gentle movement.
- Think of neighbors: use music modes for parties and calm scenes for late evening.
